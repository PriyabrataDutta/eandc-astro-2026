Why Choose a Custom UPS System for Your Business?

A standard UPS system is generally designed to accommodate common power requirements. However, industrial and commercial facilities often operate equipment with different load characteristics, operating conditions and backup expectations.

A customised Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS) system allows businesses to select a power protection solution based on their actual electrical infrastructure and operational demands.

Power Capacity Based on Your Requirements

Different facilities have different power loads. A manufacturing unit operating industrial machinery may require a different UPS capacity from a corporate office supporting servers and networking equipment.

A customised UPS configuration can be planned according to the connected load, expected operating conditions, future expansion requirements and the criticality of the equipment being protected.

Customised Battery Backup Duration

Backup requirements vary depending on the application. Some facilities need sufficient power to shut down equipment safely, while others require longer backup periods to maintain essential operations until an alternative power source becomes available.

A custom UPS solution can be configured with an appropriate battery arrangement to support the required backup duration, subject to load conditions and system specifications.

Compatibility With Existing Electrical Infrastructure

Installing a UPS system requires careful consideration of the facility's existing electrical setup.

Factors such as input voltage, output requirements, connected equipment, installation space and electrical distribution arrangements influence the selection of a suitable power backup system.

A customised solution helps businesses address these requirements without relying solely on a standard UPS configuration.

Reliable Power Protection for Critical Equipment

Unexpected power interruptions and electrical disturbances can affect sensitive electronic systems, interrupt production processes and result in operational downtime.

A properly specified UPS system provides temporary power during supply interruptions and helps protect connected equipment against supported power disturbances.

For businesses operating critical infrastructure, selecting the appropriate UPS technology and configuration is an important part of maintaining operational continuity.

Important Factors to Consider When Selecting a Custom UPS System

Choosing the right UPS involves more than determining the total power capacity. A detailed assessment of the connected equipment, installation environment and operational requirements helps identify a suitable system configuration.

Load Capacity and Power Requirements

The total electrical load is one of the primary factors in UPS selection. Businesses should evaluate the power consumption of connected equipment, starting currents, power factor and potential future load additions.

Correct UPS sizing helps prevent overloading and supports reliable operation under the intended load conditions.

Single-Phase or Three-Phase Configuration

The appropriate UPS configuration depends on the facility's electrical infrastructure and the equipment being supported.

Single-phase systems may be suitable for smaller electrical loads, while three-phase UPS systems are commonly used for larger industrial and commercial applications.

The required input and output configuration should be determined through an assessment of the installation's electrical specifications.

Battery Type and Backup Requirements

Battery selection influences backup duration, maintenance requirements, installation space and overall system cost.

The appropriate battery arrangement should be selected according to the required autonomy, connected load, charging characteristics and operating environment.

Installation Environment and Space Availability

Industrial and commercial facilities may have limitations relating to available floor space, ventilation, ambient temperature and electrical installation arrangements.

Considering these factors during UPS selection helps businesses identify a configuration suitable for their facility.

Maintenance and Long-Term Operating Costs

UPS systems require periodic inspection and maintenance to support dependable operation.

Businesses should consider maintenance accessibility, battery replacement requirements, operating efficiency and long-term service costs when selecting a customised power backup solution.
