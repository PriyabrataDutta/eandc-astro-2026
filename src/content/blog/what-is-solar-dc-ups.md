A **solar DC UPS** is a small off-grid solar system that stores solar energy in a battery and supplies **DC loads**, such as LED lights, DC fans and mobile chargers, directly from that battery. There is no inverter and no conversion to 230 V AC. It is a simple, efficient way to give lighting and basic comfort to homes, shops and huts that have no grid connection or only a few hours of supply.

## What is an off-grid solar system?

An **off-grid solar system** works independently of the electricity grid. Everything you use comes from the sun, stored in batteries for the night and for cloudy days. It has four parts:

1. **Solar panels** turn sunlight into DC electricity.
2. A **charge controller** manages charging so the battery is filled fully but not overcharged. An MPPT controller gets more energy out of the panels than a basic PWM type.
3. A **battery** stores energy for use after dark.
4. **Loads**, either DC loads connected to the battery through the controller, or AC loads through an inverter.

An off-grid system differs from an on-grid system, which feeds the grid and stops in a power cut, and from a hybrid system, which does both. Our guide to [on-grid, off-grid and hybrid solar inverters](/blogs/solar/on-grid-vs-off-grid-vs-hybrid-solar-inverter) covers those differences.

## DC loads vs AC loads

Solar panels and batteries produce DC. Most household appliances in India are built for 230 V AC. There are two ways to bridge the gap:

- **Run DC appliances directly.** DC LED bulbs, BLDC or DC fans and USB chargers are efficient and widely available. Skipping the inverter avoids its conversion losses and its standby consumption, so a small battery lasts longer.
- **Use an inverter.** An off-grid inverter converts battery DC into AC, so you can run ordinary appliances: a TV, refrigerator, mixer, pump or computer. The system is larger and costs more, but it is far more flexible.

## What a solar DC UPS is used for

- **Rural homes** without grid supply or with long outages, for lights, a fan and phone charging.
- **Shops, kiosks and farm sheds** that need light after dark.
- **Communication and monitoring equipment** at remote sites that runs on DC.
- **Schools and health sub-centres** where a few lights and fans are essential.

For outdoor lighting, an all-in-one [solar street light](/solar-street-lighting) works on the same principle: the panel charges a battery by day and the LED runs from dusk to dawn.

## DC system or off-grid AC inverter?

Choose a **solar DC system** when:

1. The loads are mainly lighting, fans and phone charging.
2. The budget is tight and the energy need is small.
3. Simplicity and low maintenance matter more than flexibility.

Choose an **off-grid AC inverter** when:

1. You need to run standard AC appliances: refrigerators, TVs, computers, pumps or motors.
2. The total load is more than a few hundred watts.
3. You may later connect to a grid or a generator as a backup charging source.

## Battery sizing basics

Size the battery from the energy you use each day, not from wattage alone.

1. **List the loads and hours.** For example, four 9 W DC LED lights for 5 hours (180 Wh), one 25 W DC fan for 8 hours (200 Wh) and phone charging (about 10 Wh). Total: about 390 Wh a day.
2. **Allow for depth of discharge.** Lead acid batteries last longer when they are not discharged deeply. Using roughly half the capacity, a 12 V system needs about 390 ÷ 12 ÷ 0.5 ≈ 65 Ah. A 75 or 80 Ah battery gives a little margin.
3. **Add days of autonomy.** For cloudy days, multiply by the number of days you want to run without sun.
4. **Size the panel to refill the battery.** Divide daily energy by the typical peak sun hours at your site, often around 4 to 5 in much of India, and allow for losses. Here, 390 ÷ (4.5 × 0.75) ≈ 115 W, so a 120 to 150 W panel is reasonable.

These are rough figures. Battery type, temperature, panel orientation and shading all change the result, so have an engineer check the design. Our guide to [UPS battery backup time](/guides/ups-battery-backup-time) explains the backup calculation in more detail.
