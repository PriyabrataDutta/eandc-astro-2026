A **UPS annual maintenance contract (AMC)** pays a service provider to keep a UPS maintained for a year. A **comprehensive AMC** usually includes preventive maintenance, breakdown visits and most spare parts, with listed exclusions. A **non-comprehensive AMC** covers the agreed preventive maintenance and visits, while spares are charged. Batteries are often excluded from both or offered separately, and **per-call service** is the pay-as-you-go option when there is no AMC at all.

The exact scope is whatever the contract says. The descriptions below are typical, not universal, so use them as a checklist when reading an offer.

## Warranty first, then AMC

A new UPS normally comes with a manufacturer's warranty against manufacturing defects. **UPS warranty service in India** generally covers repair or replacement of defective parts during the warranty period, and some makers also include preventive maintenance visits. Check:

- What the warranty period is for the UPS and, separately, for the batteries.
- Whether site visits are included or the unit must be sent back.
- Whether preventive maintenance is part of the warranty.

An AMC usually starts when the warranty ends.

## Comprehensive vs non-comprehensive AMC

| | Comprehensive AMC | Non-comprehensive AMC |
|---|---|---|
| Preventive maintenance visits | Included, at an agreed frequency | Included, at an agreed frequency |
| Breakdown visits | Usually included, often unlimited | Included or limited, as agreed |
| Labour | Included | Included for agreed visits |
| Spare parts | Most included, with exclusions | Charged separately |
| Batteries | Often excluded or a separate add-on | Usually excluded |
| Cost | Higher | Lower |
| Budget certainty | High | Parts costs vary year to year |

### What a comprehensive AMC typically excludes

Even comprehensive contracts usually exclude:

- **Batteries**, because they are consumables with a limited life.
- **Damage from external causes**: lightning, flooding, fire, rodents, wrong input supply, or tampering.
- **Physical damage** to cabinets and panels.
- **Site works**: cabling, earthing, air-conditioning and civil changes.
- **Relocation** of the UPS to a new site.
- **Software upgrades or capacity changes**, unless stated.

### When each makes sense

- **Comprehensive** suits critical loads and older UPS, where a capacitor, fan or power board failure is likely and you want a fixed annual cost.
- **Non-comprehensive** suits newer UPS, in-house electrical teams, or budgets that prefer to pay for parts only when needed.

## What preventive maintenance usually includes

A preventive maintenance visit typically covers:

1. Cleaning dust from the UPS, fans and filters.
2. Checking input, output and bypass voltages, load and alarms.
3. Checking and tightening power connections.
4. Inspecting fans, capacitors and other wear parts.
5. Checking float voltage and battery condition, often with block voltages.
6. Testing transfer to bypass and back, where safe.
7. A service report with findings and recommendations.

Our [UPS maintenance checklist](/blogs/ups-maintenance-checklist) lists what to do monthly, quarterly and yearly.

## Batteries and the AMC

Batteries are the most common cause of UPS failure and often the largest cost in a UPS's life, so treat them as their own line item:

- Check whether **battery checking** (voltage, conductance, backup test) is included even if replacement is not.
- If replacement is offered, check whether it is all batteries or only failed blocks, and which brands.
- Plan replacement separately, as in our [UPS battery replacement guide](/blogs/ups-battery-replacement-guide).

## UPS per-call services

**UPS per-call services** are for a UPS out of warranty and not under AMC. You raise a request when something goes wrong, and each visit is charged, plus any parts. It costs nothing when the UPS works, but there is no preventive maintenance, and breakdowns tend to happen at the worst time. For a non-critical UPS it can be enough; for critical loads, an AMC is usually better value.

## Questions to ask before signing

1. How many preventive visits per year, and what is checked on each?
2. Are breakdown visits unlimited or capped?
3. What spares are included, and what is the exclusion list?
4. Are batteries included, checked only, or excluded?
5. What is the response commitment for a breakdown, in writing?
6. Is remote monitoring included or available?
7. Does the provider have engineers and spares near your site?
