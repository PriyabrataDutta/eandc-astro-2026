Businesses across India are adding EV chargers for staff, customers and their own fleets. The right charger depends on how long vehicles stay parked, which vehicles you expect, and how much power your site can supply. Here is how to decide.

## Step 1: AC or DC?

**AC chargers** send alternating current to the vehicle, and the car's onboard charger converts it to DC for the battery. They are simpler, cheaper and need less electrical capacity. The catch is that the car's onboard charger sets the speed: many cars in India accept roughly 3 to 11 kW on AC, so a bigger AC charger will not charge them faster.

**DC chargers** convert the power to DC inside the charger and feed the battery directly, bypassing the onboard charger. They charge many times faster but cost more and need a much bigger electrical supply.

| Where | Typical stay | Suggested charger |
|---|---|---|
| Office, factory staff parking | 6 to 9 hours | AC, 3 to 7.4 kW |
| Hotels, malls, hospitals | 1 to 4 hours | AC 11 to 22 kW, or a small DC charger |
| Fleet depots (cabs, delivery) | Short turnaround | DC, 30 to 60 kW |
| Highways, public hubs | 15 to 45 minutes | DC, 60 to 240 kW |

## Step 2: Choose the connector

- **Type 2 (AC):** the common AC connector for electric cars in India.
- **CCS2 (DC):** the common DC fast charging connector on new electric cars in India.
- **CHAdeMO (DC):** found on some older imported models.
- **GB/T-based:** used on some light and commercial EVs, and by the low-power Bharat DC-001 standard.
- **Bharat AC-001:** an early low-power AC standard with 15 A sockets, still found at some older public sites.

If you serve the public or a mixed fleet, a charger with two guns, and different connectors on each, covers more vehicles from one unit.

## Step 3: Check your electricity connection

This is the step most often missed. Add up the chargers you plan, and check them against your sanctioned load and transformer capacity:

- A few 7 kW AC chargers usually fit within a commercial connection.
- A 60 kW DC charger draws as much as a small factory line, and may need a higher sanctioned load or a new connection from your DISCOM.
- Smart load management can share the available power between chargers so the site never exceeds its limit.

Ask an electrical engineer to check cable sizes, protection and earthing before installation. A solar plant with a [hybrid inverter](/solar-hybrid-inverter) can offset some of the daytime charging energy.

## Step 4: Decide how you will manage and bill

A smart, connected charger lets you:

- Control who can charge, with RFID cards or an app.
- See usage per user or per vehicle, and bill for it.
- Limit power at peak times to control demand charges.
- Get alerts when a charger faults.

If you plan to join a public charging network or app, check that the charger can integrate with it before you buy. Open standards such as OCPP make this easier.

## Step 5: Plan installation and service

- Place chargers where cables reach parking bays without trailing across walkways.
- Use outdoor-rated enclosures for open parking.
- Plan for future chargers now: laying extra conduit and cable costs far less during the first installation.
- Make sure local service is available, because a broken charger is worse than none for customers.
