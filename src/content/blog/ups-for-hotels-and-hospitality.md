**Online UPS for hotels and hospitality** keeps the systems guests notice first: check-in, key cards, payments, Wi-Fi and the lights and sound in a banquet hall. A hotel's DG handles lighting, air conditioning and kitchens; the online UPS bridges the seconds before the DG takes load and protects electronics from voltage swings. A typical hotel uses a central three phase UPS for its IT room and a few smaller units for front office, restaurants and banquets.

## A hotel's critical load map

Hotels mix guest-facing, back-of-house and building systems. Walking through each department is the fastest way to build the load list.

### Front office and lobby

- Front desk PCs, card readers and passport scanners
- Key card encoders
- Lobby Wi-Fi access points and digital signage
- Concierge and bell desk phones (IP telephony)

### IT room

- Property management system (PMS) server or local gateway to a cloud PMS
- Internet links, firewall and core switches
- IP telephony (PBX) and voicemail
- CCTV recorders and access control server

### Restaurants, bars and banquets

- POS terminals, kitchen display systems and order printers
- Banquet audio-visual: mixers, amplifiers, projectors and stage lighting controllers
- Coffee machines with digital controls only where specified; most F&B equipment stays on DG

### Building systems

- BMS controllers, lift controllers' communication interface and fire control room PCs
- Guest room door lock gateways and energy management controllers

## Which loads stay on DG

Guest room AC, chillers, laundry, kitchen ranges, pool pumps, lifts and general lighting all stay on DG. They are large, motor-driven or resistive loads, and a short gap while the DG starts does no real harm. The exceptions are **emergency and escape lighting**, which normally have their own battery system as required by the applicable codes, and lift **rescue devices**, which are specified by the lift maker.

## Banquet halls: a distinct case

A banquet hall during a wedding or conference is the most visible place a power cut can happen. A few seconds of silence from the sound system while the DG starts is noticeable to every guest.

- Put the **audio mixer, amplifiers, wireless mic receivers and projector** on an online UPS.
- Keep **stage lighting dimmers and LED walls** on DG unless the event designer specifies otherwise. They draw high power and are hard to back up economically.
- Because banquet AV is often brought in by event companies, provide **clearly labelled UPS outlets** so they do not plug high loads into them.

## Sizing and backup

Hotel IT loads are moderate but run 24 hours a day, 365 days a year. Steps:

1. Total the IT room, front office, POS and banquet AV loads separately.
2. Decide whether one central UPS feeds everything through distribution boards, or whether the banquet and restaurants get their own units.
3. Set backup to cover DG start and changeover with margin, plus enough to shut down servers cleanly if the DG fails.
4. Add margin for later additions such as more rooms, a spa or a new restaurant.

The [UPS battery backup time](/guides/ups-battery-backup-time) guide shows how to work out runtime.

## Redundancy without overspending

A mid-size hotel can usually live with one central UPS plus a static and maintenance bypass, as long as a service contract keeps it healthy. Larger and luxury properties, or hotels that host conferences, often add a second UPS in **hot standby or parallel** for the IT room. A [static transfer switch](/blogs/what-is-a-static-transfer-switch) can also let two different UPS units feed one critical load.

## Noise, space and appearance

Hotels care more about these than most buildings.

- **Noise:** UPS fans and transformer hum should not reach guest rooms or the spa. Keep the UPS in a plant or electrical room with sound-separated walls.
- **Space:** basements are often shared with car parking and services. Plan a ventilated UPS and battery room early in the design, not after fit-out.
- **Rack mount units** suit floor IT closets where a tower UPS would not fit; see [what is a rack mount UPS](/blogs/what-is-rack-mount-ups).
- **Battery rooms:** keep them ventilated and dry, with access only for engineering staff.

## Monitoring for the engineering team

The chief engineer needs to know when a UPS is on battery or in alarm before the front desk does. An SNMP card on each UPS lets the IT and engineering teams see status from a browser and receive email or SMS alerts. Groups with many properties can watch every hotel from one place. Our post on [SNMP and remote UPS monitoring](/blogs/snmp-and-remote-ups-monitoring) explains the set-up.

## Service

Hotels run around the clock, so maintenance has to fit around occupancy. Schedule preventive maintenance and battery tests in low-occupancy periods, and confirm how quickly service can reach your location.
