Tubular, GEL and AGM are all lead-acid batteries. They use the same basic chemistry but differ in how the plates and the acid are built, and that changes how well they handle deep discharge, heat and neglect. Here is how each one works and where it fits.

## How does a tubular battery work?

A **tubular battery** gets its name from the positive plate. Instead of a flat grid pasted with active material, it uses a row of lead-alloy spines. Each spine sits inside a porous tube, usually woven polyester, and the space around it is filled with lead oxide active material.

When any lead-acid battery discharges and recharges, the positive active material expands and contracts. In a flat-plate battery, this slowly loosens the material and it sheds to the bottom of the cell, which is a main cause of wear. In a tubular plate, the tube holds the material tight against the spine, so much less is lost. That is why tubular batteries handle repeated **deep discharges** far better than flat-plate batteries.

The negative plate is a normal flat pasted plate. Most tubular batteries sold in India for inverters and UPS are **flooded**: the plates sit in free liquid sulphuric acid, with removable vent caps on top.

### Maintenance

Flooded batteries lose water during charging as gas escapes through the vents. They need:

- Topping up with distilled water every few months, following the level indicator
- A ventilated location, because charging gives off small amounts of hydrogen
- Clean, tight terminals and an occasional check of specific gravity

Skip the topping up and the plates dry out and fail early.

## What is an AGM battery?

**AGM (absorbent glass mat)** batteries are sealed VRLA batteries. The acid is soaked into a fine glass-fibre mat squeezed between flat plates, with no free liquid. Gas produced during charging recombines inside, so there is no water topping. AGM has low internal resistance and delivers high current well, which suits online UPS loads with backup from a few minutes to a couple of hours. Most SMF batteries are AGM.

## What is a GEL battery?

**GEL** batteries are also sealed VRLA batteries, but the acid is mixed with fumed silica into a thick gel. The gel conducts heat better than a glass mat and resists drying out, so GEL batteries usually tolerate **deep cycling** and **higher temperatures** better than AGM. The trade-off is lower peak current and a need for correct charging voltage, as overcharging can damage the gel.

### GEL vs AGM in one line

Choose AGM for high-rate, short-backup UPS duty; choose GEL for deeper, more frequent cycling where the battery must still be sealed. For more on how sealed batteries work, see [SMF vs VRLA battery](/blogs/batteries/smf-vs-vrla-battery).

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Flooded tubular | AGM (VRLA) | GEL (VRLA) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electrolyte | Free liquid acid | Acid in glass mat | Acid in silica gel |
| Deep-cycle ability | Very good | Moderate | Good |
| Maintenance | Water topping | None | None |
| Ventilation | Needed | Minimal | Minimal |
| Heat tolerance | Good | Weakest of the three | Better than AGM |
| High-current delivery | Moderate | Best | Lower |
| Upfront cost | Lowest per Ah | Moderate | Highest |

These are general tendencies; individual models vary, so check the maker's datasheet for cycle life at your depth of discharge and temperature. Like all lead-acid batteries, each type loses life quickly when it runs hot, typically about half for every 8 to 10°C above 25°C.

## Which battery for which use?

### Home and shop inverters

Indian homes and shops can see daily power cuts of an hour or more, and the battery discharges deeply each time. **Flooded tubular** is the usual choice: it handles deep cycling, tolerates heat and costs the least. Just keep up with the water topping.

### Online UPS

UPS batteries usually sit on float for months and then deliver high current for a short time. **AGM (SMF)** suits this well, is sealed, and fits inside UPS cabinets and racks. Where backup runs for several hours, or outages are long and frequent, tubular or GEL may last longer. For battery sizing, use our [UPS battery backup time](/guides/ups-battery-backup-time) guide.

### Solar

A solar battery charges by day and discharges at night, every day. That calls for a deep-cycle battery: **flooded tubular** where maintenance is possible, or **GEL** at remote and unmanned sites where water topping is not practical. Lithium is also a strong option for daily cycling; see [lithium vs lead-acid](/blogs/batteries/lithium-vs-lead-acid-ups-battery). E&C's [off-grid solar inverters](/solar-off-grid-inverter) work with battery storage for such systems.

### Telecom and remote sites

Sealed batteries avoid site visits for topping up. GEL is often favoured in hot shelters because of its better heat tolerance.

## Quick decision guide

1. Long, frequent outages and someone to top up water? **Tubular.**
2. Short to medium backup inside a UPS cabinet? **AGM / SMF.**
3. Daily cycling, sealed, warm or remote site? **GEL.**
4. Want the longest life and least maintenance, with budget to match? Consider **lithium**.
