Almost every UPS in India still runs on lead-acid batteries, either sealed maintenance-free (SMF, also called VRLA) or flooded tubular. Lithium batteries, mostly lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4), are now a practical alternative. They cost more upfront, so the real question is whether they pay for themselves over the life of the UPS.

## Lithium vs lead-acid at a glance

| | SMF / VRLA lead-acid | Tubular lead-acid | Lithium (LiFePO4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical life at 25°C | 3 to 5 years | 5 to 8 years | 8 to 10 years |
| Charge cycles | A few hundred deep cycles | More than SMF | 2,000 or more |
| Weight for the same energy | Heaviest | Heavy | Roughly a third to half |
| Recharge time after an outage | Slow, often 8 hours or more | Slow | Fast, often a few hours |
| Maintenance | None (sealed) | Water topping, ventilation | None; BMS monitors cells |
| Heat tolerance | Life drops sharply above 25°C | Better than SMF | Much better |
| Upfront cost | Lowest | Low | Highest |

The life and cycle figures are typical design values from battery makers' datasheets at 25°C; check the datasheet of the exact model you are quoted. Real life depends on temperature, how often and how deeply the batteries discharge, and how well they are charged. For backup time calculations, see [UPS battery backup time](/guides/ups-battery-backup-time).

## Where lithium wins

### Longer life and fewer replacements

A lead-acid bank in a warm Indian server room often needs replacing every 3 to 4 years. A LiFePO4 bank typically lasts 8 to 10 years and 2,000 or more cycles, so over ten years you may buy lithium once against lead-acid two or three times, plus the labour and downtime of each changeover.

### Heat tolerance

Lead-acid batteries are rated at 25°C, and their life falls quickly in heat: as a rule of thumb, it roughly halves for every 8 to 10°C above that. Many sites in India cannot keep the battery area at 25°C all year. LiFePO4 handles higher temperatures with much less loss of life, which can also reduce the air-conditioning the battery room needs.

### Weight and space

For the same stored energy, a lithium pack weighs roughly a third to half as much as lead-acid and takes less space. That matters on upper floors, in rack-mounted systems and where floor loading is limited.

### Faster recharge

Lithium accepts charge much faster. After an outage, the UPS is ready for the next one sooner, which matters in areas with frequent power cuts.

### Built-in monitoring

Every lithium pack has a battery management system (BMS) that balances the cells and protects against overcharge, deep discharge and overheating. Many also report state of charge and health, so a failing battery is spotted before an outage, not during one.

## Where lead-acid still makes sense

- **Lowest upfront cost.** If the budget is fixed today, SMF batteries cost the least.
- **Existing UPS that does not support lithium.** Changing chemistry needs a lithium-compatible UPS and charger settings.
- **Long-duration, low-cycle backup.** Large tubular banks for hours of backup at sites with few outages can still be the economical choice.

## How to compare the total cost

1. Price the battery bank for the backup time you need in each chemistry.
2. Estimate how many times each will be replaced over 10 years at your battery room temperature.
3. Add installation, disposal and downtime for each replacement.
4. Add any savings in cooling and floor space.

Lithium tends to come out ahead where lead-acid would be replaced two or more times in ten years: warm battery rooms, frequent outages and deep daily cycling. Where batteries sit in a cool room and rarely discharge, lead-acid lasts longer and the gap narrows. Ask for both quotations and run the four steps above with your own figures.
